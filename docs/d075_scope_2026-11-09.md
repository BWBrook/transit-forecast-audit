## 1. The negative result is structural, not a weak rule

Evidence: `scripts/t020_review.py`, written separately from Codex's `t020_*`
scripts. It reads Codex's saved event tables and writes
`results/t020_review/`.

**Even hindsight cannot win.** I ranked planets by their *realised* mean
absolute DRC error, which no real rule could know before the event. That
"oracle" was put through the same contest as the real rules: flag 10% of
planets, pads of 15–240 min, and the same two equal-budget comparators. It
still loses to both comparators, in every era, at every pad:

| Slice | Oracle loss vs proportional | Oracle loss vs additive |
|---|---:|---:|
| Earlier (S56–S73) | 16 to 37 points | 24 to 38 points |
| 2026 | 11 to 31 points | 22 to 33 points |
| Pooled | 11 to 34 points | 23 to 35 points |

All losses are percentage points of planet-balanced capture.

**A ceiling that needs no ranking at all says the same.** Even if a rule
captured every event of every flagged planet, it could add at most 10–11
points to the baseline. Compare that with the weaker comparator at the
smallest budget any 15-minute triage could spend:

| Slice | Baseline | Triage ceiling | Weaker comparator |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Earlier | 0.557 | 0.665 | 0.631 |
| 2026 | 0.623 | 0.725 | 0.710 |
| Pooled | 0.598 | 0.700 | 0.693 |

At best, then, a rule would win by about 1.5 points, and only in that
impossible extreme case. No useful gain is attainable in this population.

**The reason is that there is nothing to flag.** In this DRC-core population:

| | Earlier slice | 2026 slice |
|---|---:|---:|
| Median absolute DRC error | 1.0 min | 1.2 min |
| 99th percentile | 10.6 min | 25.6 min |
| Maximum | 30.4 min | 36.6 min |
| Events over 60 min | 0 | 0 |
| Median issued forecast SD | 0.47 min | 0.82 min |
| Median measurement SD / forecast SD | 2.7 | 1.7 |

The worst planets are GJ 1252 b and HD 110113 b. Forecast-only windows of
±1.96 SD capture 0.56–0.62 of measured midpoints. Adding the measurement SD
raises that to 0.88–0.93. So most "misses" are measurement noise falling
outside windows only minutes wide. A pad of 1–3 minutes spread across every
planet fixes them. Targeted hours-long pads cannot.

**This was my design error.** I proposed (D073) measured-midpoint capture
with forecast-only windows as the endpoint. In a population where the
measurement SD exceeds the forecast SD and no forecast fails grossly, that
endpoint rewards uniform padding by construction. Codex's T020 implemented
it faithfully, and the negative result stands. I accept D074's endpoint
qualifications, and its warning not to retune thresholds, pads or the cohort
until something wins.

## 2. Gross misses across everything inspected

Evidence: `results/t020_review/drd_2026_gross_misses.json`. A gross miss here
means an error over 60 min **and** over 3 forecast SDs.

- **The frozen 2026 DRD test:** exactly one planet of 151 has a gross miss.
  It is **HR 858 b**: all 16 of its events, up to 404 min. HR 858 b is not in
  DRC. The next-largest maximum error on any planet is 26 min (GJ 1252 b).
- **DRC-era development:** none in 128 core planets, with forecasts issued in
  September 2022 and tested on 2022–2026 events.

One positive case cannot train or validate a warning rule. Opening S74–S97
to test this candidate is therefore not justified, and I withdraw that part
of D071/D073.

S74–S97 could still answer a different, descriptive question: how often
issued forecasts fail grossly on new hosts. If none of about 183 hosts
failed, the 95% upper bound on the rate would be about 1.6%. That is a
modest gain over the present 1 in 151, and it would need the full freeze
and access machinery. It is not needed for this paper; I record it as an
option for later.

## 3. The question the evidence actually answers

The work answers a better-posed question than the one it set out with: **how
reliable are issued ExoClock transit forecasts, where do they fail, and does
a population-calibrated widening of their intervals help?**

1. **Primary frozen result (unchanged).** The population correction M2
   versus the issued intervals M0 is inconclusive. The standardised IS95
   difference is −0.121, joint interval [−1.84, 2.20]. With the HR 858 b
   sector 105 measurement sensitivity it is −0.089, interval [−1.880, +1.124]
   (C052, D074). The sign depends on the loss scale and on one planet.
2. **Accuracy.** Issued forecasts are accurate to minutes. Under DRD in 2026,
   150 of 151 planets had every accepted timing within 26 min, with a median
   absolute error near 1 min. DRC forecasts issued in 2022 stayed within
   37 min for all 128 core planets through 2026.
3. **Calibration.** Issued SDs are small relative to realised errors plus
   measurement noise. Planet-balanced convolved 95% coverage was 0.908 under M0 (C043). The
   frozen correction reached 0.976 at about 1.7 times the width. In absolute
   terms the misses are minutes.
4. **The single failure is real and was visible.** HR 858 b's six-hour miss
   is a physical TTV, confirmed by independent photometry with b early, c late
   and d on time (C050, C051, C057). Its warning signs could be seen at issue:
   - it first appears in DRD;
   - its 16 input timings end in 2020 and span 756 days;
   - their scatter about the issued line is 2.2 times the stated errors
     (standardised RMS);
   - published TTVs exist (Bonfanti et al. 2025).
5. **Triage is not learnable at this rarity.** In the DRC core, even a
   hindsight ranking cannot beat uniform padding. The practical advice is
   uniform: a pad of a few minutes is negligible beside a transit's duration.
   Human review should go to the rare ephemerides with old, sparse or
   TTV-flagged inputs. That review is a recommendation, not a tested rule.
6. **Methods outputs:**
   - the frozen protocol, with its deviation recorded;
   - two independent timing checkers and their adjudication;
   - the portable benchmark that replays the scores exactly;
   - the release-information ledger.

**Proposed working title:** *Issued exoplanet transit forecasts are accurate
to minutes but fail rarely and visibly: a frozen test of ExoClock ephemerides
with TESS.*
