## Method, scope and integrity

The new engine imports no project measurement, acceptance or score code.
Astropy BLS is the shared standard search library; the phase-blind search,
continuum estimate, empirical trapezoid and nuisance profiling were written
independently. Catalogue period is used to identify a detected train; an issued
epoch is never used to find or fit it. Per-transit linear baseline and depth
are profiled, and influential non-Gaussian cases also receive local free-shape
sensitivity fits. SAP and PDCSAP are reduction sensitivities of shared photons.
There is no pixel/centroid analysis or newly qualified uncertainty model.

The amended run processed all 35 products and both flux routes in 194 seconds
on two workers. All input hashes match saved metadata, all time headers are
TDB/SOLARSYSTEM with BJD reference 2457000, and no product or flux-processing
exception occurred. There were zero downloads. Filenames outside S46–S73 or
S98 onward are rejected before opening. The original design and amended
source bytes are checked against their commits before execution.

The first complete run took 145 seconds. Its duplicate-target/harmonic masks
could remove a target's own transit. The amendment preserves target cadences
from such residual masks while retaining opposite-phase/nonharmonic masking;
it does not establish that every apparent companion is physical. This repairs
WASP-18b PDCSAP from zero to 22/22 timing matches and WASP-126b from 2/7 to 7/7.
The full 41-file first-attempt archive remains hash-verifiable, ignored on
disk, with a compact tracked receipt. It was not silently replaced.

## Zero-accepted and covered-failure cases

HIP94235b, TOI-251b and TOI-269b were not independently identified in the
selected product on either flux route. TOI-262b has two informative PDCSAP
timing candidates; TOI-4308b has two informative candidates out of three.
Neither candidate train is identified on SAP. These are unresolved light-curve
candidates, not recovered truth events, known forecast misses or corrected
primary outcomes. Limited search/model scope and stellar/instrumental structure
remain possible explanations for non-detection.

The structured failure products cover edge, integration, likelihood-support,
identification and partial-coverage failures. Four categories include
prediction-qualified failed events. The excluded-coverage example is explicitly
unqualified at the prediction, a partial-coverage contrast rather than evidence
of broad cadence coverage. All independent candidate times and their later
nearest-manifest labels, including unresolved cycle labels, are saved in
`unscored_candidates.csv`. Those labels are metadata comparisons after blind
fitting, not an independent proof of identity or a population capture bound.

## C050 and next scientific action

The accompanying code review distinguishes independent light-curve analysis
from independent pixel extraction. C050's shared-photon agreement, opposite
b/c residuals and approximately on-time d constrain a common additive clock
error and support known TTVs; they do not by themselves prove a unique physical
mechanism or exclude all target-specific extraction errors. Its reported
sqrt-summed-variance z-RMS is an agreement index, not a calibration test.

The highest-value next step is an independent adjudication of the remaining
coverage/alias and HR858 shape flags, preserving these fixed-sample outputs,
before claiming that influential measurement validation is closed. No new
sample, frozen correction or access to S74–S97 follows automatically.
