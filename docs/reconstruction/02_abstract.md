# Reconstruction, Step 1b: abstract (under 150 words)

Working draft, 10 November 2026, by Claude (Opus 5.5), from
`01_working_abstract.md`. Numbers from C043, C050, C054 and C063. It can be
expanded towards the 250-word AAS limit later.

Observers schedule exoplanet transits from catalogue ephemerides, yet the stated uncertainties are rarely tested against later, independent timings. We compare one issued ExoClock release with 2,185 TESS transit times of 151 planets, measured blind to the forecasts under a protocol frozen before the data were opened, and release it as a reusable per-planet table. The forecasts were accurate: the median timing error was 1.1 minutes, and every measured transit of 150 planets fell within 26.4 minutes. Nominal 95% intervals, combined with measurement uncertainty, covered about 91% of timings per planet. A correction learned from the previous release widened the intervals, with inconclusive effect on a proper interval score. The one large failure, HR 858 b, arrived six hours early, consistent with transit-timing variations in a multi-planet system whose ephemeris rested on old, scattered inputs. Such ephemerides could be flagged, but one case cannot validate a warning rule.
