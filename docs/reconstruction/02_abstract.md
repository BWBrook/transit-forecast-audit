Observers schedule exoplanet transits from catalogue predictions and their stated uncertainties, but refitting a catalogue does not test how its earlier predictions performed. We compare one issued release of the ExoClock catalogue with 2,185 transit times of 151 planets later measured by TESS, under measurement, acceptance and scoring rules frozen before those data were opened, and provide the comparison as a reusable per-planet table. The pooled median absolute error was 1.1 minutes, and every accepted timing of 150 planets fell within 26.4 minutes of prediction. Nominal 95% intervals, combined with measurement uncertainty, covered about 91% of timings per planet. A correction learned from the previous release widened the intervals; its prespecified interval-score comparison was inconclusive. The one large failure, HR 858 b, arrived six hours early, consistent with timing variations in a multi-planet system whose inputs were old and scattered. One case cannot validate a warning rule.
