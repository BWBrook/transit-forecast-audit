Observers schedule exoplanet transits from catalogue predictions. We compare one issued ExoClock release with 2,185 accepted TESS timings of 151 planets, using measurement and acceptance rules frozen before held-out access. The planned comparison table provides a reusable benchmark. The pooled median absolute error was 1.1 minutes; every accepted timing of 150 planets fell within 26.4 minutes. Nominal 95% intervals, combined with measurement uncertainty, covered about 91% of timings per planet. A correction learned from the previous release widened the intervals; its prespecified interval-score comparison was inconclusive. The one large failure, HR 858 b, arrived six hours early, consistent with timing variations in a multi-planet system with old, scattered inputs. One case cannot validate a warning rule. The benchmark records accepted measurements and the full opportunity ledger, supporting further comparisons under explicit measurement and selection assumptions.
