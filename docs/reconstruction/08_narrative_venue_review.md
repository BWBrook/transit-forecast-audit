## Venue: lean ApJS for the reference product; keep AJ open

The official [ApJS scope](https://journals.aas.org/astrophysical-supplement-series/)
includes extensive research and reference catalogues/data compilations. That
supports the product framing. I recommend ApJS if the deliverable is a curated
benchmark others can use: all intended planets, selection/status information,
source identity and uncertainty conventions, with the accepted-event scoring
package underneath it. A small summary table plus a DOI is not by itself the
scientific case for that venue.

The official [AJ scope](https://journals.aas.org/astronomical-journal/) includes
observational results, surveys, analysis methods and software. A focused frozen
evaluation can fit it too. Four single-system papers among five chosen examples
do not establish that AJ excludes multi-system calibration studies. The
original note's universal absence claim is withdrawn. Scope statements support
fit judgments, not predictions of editorial acceptance.

The five-paper comparisons are useful examples, not a representative survey
or formal norms. Their reference counts prompt a substantive prior-art check,
not a target of hundreds of citations. Add sources that explain the contribution,
source data and limitations, without padding the list.

The [AAS preparation guide](https://journals.aas.org/manuscript-preparation/)
sets a single-paragraph abstract maximum of 250 words; it does not require
250 words. Barry's under-150-word exercise is compatible. LLM use must be
acknowledged/cited. The [AAS Data Guide](https://journals.aas.org/data-guide/)
recommends structured machine-readable products and permits raw delimited
tables at submission, with conversion and author verification later. Initial
CDS formatting is not a prerequisite for this reconstruction.

Keep the claims ledger and repository links. Journal-facing removal of inline
claim IDs is a later rendering choice, not removal of provenance. Venue
remains deferred as Barry requested. Converge on the message and product
scope, then take Barry's step 3; no new data study or venue-specific manuscript
conversion is needed first.
