# Reconstruction, Step 1c: main message (25 words or fewer)

Working draft, 10 November 2026, by Claude (Opus 5.5), distilled from
`01_working_abstract.md` and `02_abstract.md`. Evidence: C063 and C050.

> Published predictions of when distant planets pass in front of their stars were accurate to minutes when tested later; one in 151 missed by hours.

(25 words.) Written for a reader who knows nothing of ExoClock, TESS or
ephemerides. Revised after Barry's comment on the first version ("too much
wood for trees. Don't assume anything of your reader"), which read: "Tested on
later, independent TESS timings, ExoClock's issued forecasts were accurate to
minutes for 150 of 151 planets; the one exception missed by six hours."

**Codex's scope note, 10 November.** The quoted version above remains Barry's
chosen draft. "One in 151" describes the accepted sample, not a catalogue-wide
failure rate. An alternative for consideration (25 words):

> Predictions of when planets pass in front of their stars usually agreed with later measurements to minutes; one of 151 tested planets missed by hours.

Whichever wording we choose, the abstract and sample table must identify the
151 planets with accepted timings, from 156 planned planets.

**Claude's reconciled proposal, 10 November (25 words).** It takes Codex's
scope anchor, "agreed with later measurements" and "151 tested planets". It
keeps "Published", because the forecasts' being issued is the point of the
test. It drops "usually", because all 150 planets were within 26.4 minutes.

> Published predictions of when planets pass in front of their stars agreed with later measurements to minutes; one of 151 tested planets missed by hours.

Barry chooses among the three.

## How every other line of evidence serves it

- **The per-planet table is the product.** It is how the message can be
  checked and reused, not a second message.
- **The frozen protocol and independent observations** distinguish the test
  from a refit. The search uses the catalogue period and acceptance uses a
  quarter-period forecast gate; neither complete blindness nor complete
  recovery follows.
- **Calibration**: about 91% coverage of accepted measured timings by nominal
  95% intervals. This qualifies the accuracy statement. It tests combined
  forecast-and-measurement uncertainty under the covariance assumptions.
- **The inconclusive correction** shows that widening every interval does not
  clearly help under the prespecified score. The result does not establish
  why the correction failed to show a benefit.
- **HR 858 b's re-timing and release features** show the exception is real,
  and has antecedent warning features. They describe one case; they are not a tested
  rule.
- **The DRC 2022 forecasts**, within 37 minutes for all 128 planets, extend
  the descriptive comparison to selected development blocks reaching about
  3.94 years after issue, not continuous coverage or a second untouched test.

## Left out of this paper

- **The search for a warning rule (T020).** It found no benefit within the
  tested fixed allocation policy and supplied outcomes. It does not rule out
  all warning rules or establish a population failure rate.
- **A gross-failure rate from sectors 74–97.** That would be a different,
  later paper, if Barry authorises opening those data.
